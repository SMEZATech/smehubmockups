Uppercase type below roughly 9–10px stops being reliably legible on screen — red is below that floor, amber is marginal, green is comfortable.

This is the whole argument in one table. V1’s wordmark renders 2.27× larger than V2 or V3 in the same avatar, because its lockup is 2.70:1 rather than 6.9:1. A wide one-line lockup has to shrink until its whole height fits across the middle of the circle, and the type goes with it.

A note on the data

Real and verified from the live profiles: account names, all handles, the LinkedIn tagline, industry, location, employee band, founding year, and the LinkedIn follower count of 9,456. The site tagline is quoted from the About page.

Shown as “—”: follower and post counts for X, Instagram and Facebook, which were not retrievable (X returned HTTP 402; Facebook served almost nothing). Left blank rather than invented — no metric here is made up.

Platform layouts are faithful approximations for judging the logo, not pixel-exact clones. Cap heights are computed from the real vector geometry at each platform’s displayed avatar size, with a 6% margin inside the largest shape that fits the crop.
